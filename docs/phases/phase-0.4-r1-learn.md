## What worked (specific)

1. **Writing the scorer before the curves existed, and committing it with its reasoning.** Four
   choices hide inside "steps to 50% saturation" and each moves the number. Committing
   `saturation.py` while all nine runs were still training meant none of them could be chosen to
   suit the answer — and the git timestamp is the evidence. The discipline held; §3.4 of the retro
   is about a different failure mode entirely, and the pre-commitment is what made that failure
   *visible* rather than invisible.

2. **The pre-registered third branch on G2 fired and saved the phase.** G2's plan wrote down what to
   do if base rates came back at zero. They did — 0/4096 on arithmetic — and the substrate switched
   to creative writing without any argument about whether the result was "really" a failure. A
   branch written in advance turned a dead end into a 20-minute pivot.

3. **Doubling the seeds instead of interpreting n=3 harder.** Batch 1 gave p = 0.10 in the predicted
   direction, which is exactly the result that invites one more analysis pass. Running six more at
   `$0` was cheaper than the argument, and it reversed the direction.

4. **Deriving the fix from the actual failure rather than the obvious knob.** The censored evals
   looked like a step-budget problem. Measuring found eval taking ~2h30m and lagging 7–12 steps, so
   the fix was eval size and cadence — and it removed censoring completely, including in a run that
   aborted at step 39. `max_steps` would have reproduced the problem at greater cost.

5. **Making the generator reproduce the nine existing configs byte-for-byte before using it.** The
   configs had claimed *"Generated by scripts/gen_r1_configs.py"* since they were written, and that
   script had never been committed. `--check` reproducing all nine is what licensed deriving batch 2
   from verified training parameters rather than a hand-copied template.

6. **Verifying `max_steps` does not touch the learning rate before claiming batch 2 was
   measurement-only.** Read `prime-rl`'s source: the default is `ConstantSchedulerConfig`, and
   `max_steps` enters the scheduler only under `linear` or `cosine`. Had it been cosine, the claim
   in the batch-2 commit would have been false and the batches unpoolable.

## What caused friction

1. **The nine-run design could not answer its own question, and nobody noticed for a day.** The
   floor at 3 vs 3 is one line of arithmetic (`1/C(6,3) = 0.05`) available before any run. It was
   computed only after batch 2 forced the question.

2. **A false positive from a scorer written to prevent exactly that.** `r1p_confirmed: True` on a
   p = 0.29 null. Caught only because batch 2 reversed the direction — had batch 2 agreed with batch
   1, the phase would have shipped a confirmed R1-P.

3. **The first fix was worse than the problem in the long run.** Non-overlap of seed ranges avoided
   choosing α post-hoc and is anti-consistent: power against a real 1σ effect falls from 26% at n=3
   to 0.05% at n=12. It survived one commit before simulation killed it.

4. **Four runs read as `FAILED` and were nearly written off as infra flakes.** Reading the logs took
   ten minutes and inverted the reading entirely.

5. **`prime train metrics` is not stable immediately after a run goes terminal.** `--check` failed
   against data that was not wrong, only early — trailing eval rows were still flushing. A
   re-fetch-and-diff resolved it, but "committed data doesn't match the API" and "the API hasn't
   settled" produce identical output.

6. **Two monitors were needed** — the first expired at its 1h timeout minutes before the last run
   landed. Persistent monitors for anything measured in hours.

7. **`prime train metrics` is 1.5–2.4 MB × 600 keys per run**, and fetching 15 of them takes long
   enough to exceed a 120 s tool timeout twice. Worth batching or caching next time.

## Rule changes

### `[ADD]`

1. **A directional comparison states its design floor — `p_floor = 1/C(n_a+n_b, n_a)` against the
   intended α — in the plan, beside the seed count.**
   *Trigger: without this, nine runs are spent on a comparison whose best attainable p equals the
   threshold and can never clear it (R1 batch 1, 2026-08-05); with this, the seed count is derived
   from the claim being made rather than from convention.*

2. **Seeds launched in one wave count as one draw for variance purposes. Estimate seed variance
   across launch waves, or declare the figure a lower bound.**
   *Trigger: without this, `ocean`'s three same-wave seeds report sd 2.64 against a two-wave 6.34
   and a tight triple from a high-variance arm reads as a real effect; with this, "±sd over 3 seeds"
   states whether those seeds could vary independently.*

3. **Every pre-registered decision table has an explicit indeterminate row with its own action.**
   *Trigger: without this, an outcome space of {CONFIRMED, FALSIFIED} forces a scorer to return the
   nearest available cell when the truth is "not resolved", and pre-registration does not catch it
   because the analysis was genuinely fixed in advance; with this, "unresolved" is a pre-registered
   result rather than an omission the code has to improvise around.*

4. **A field in a results dict is named for what it computes, not for the claim it serves.**
   *Trigger: without this, `r1p_confirmed` holds a median-ordering check and every reader — including
   its author — treats it as the verdict; with this, `r1p_ordering_holds` cannot be misread and the
   real test has to be written.*

5. **A criterion that avoids a threshold is simulated against a known effect before it is adopted.**
   *Trigger: without this, non-overlap-of-ranges looks assumption-free and ships with power that
   falls to 0.05% by n=12, because its assumptions are hidden in the sample size; with this, the
   rule's operating characteristics are measured before it becomes a gate.*

6. **Platform run status is a hypothesis; the curve is the data.**
   *Trigger: without this, four runs marked `FAILED: BackoffLimitExceeded` are discarded as infra
   noise when they are the phase's cleanest saturations, terminated for learning too completely;
   with this, terminal status is a prompt to read the logs rather than a verdict.*

### `[MODIFY]`

7. **EXECUTED — `P-seeds`.** Was *"1 seed × all exploratory · 3 seeds × 4 confirmatory."* Now carries
   *"≥4 per arm for any directional comparison"* (6 if the arm is high-variance), because n=3 cannot
   clear α = 0.05. Exploratory arms are unaffected — they carry no directional claim.

8. **EXECUTED — `P-alpha` added**, and then **corrected the same day** (see 17): the original pin
   said "0.05 one-sided" while the rule tested both tails at full α, and reported `powered` in place
   of power. Post-hoc in form, not in consequence: R1's p is 0.24–0.29 and fails at 0.05, 0.10 and
   0.20 alike, which is what makes adopting it now legitimate. It binds from Walk onward.

9. **EXECUTED — L1 annotated as a filter, not a ranking**, and the annotation was ~~half right~~
   **half wrong**. The "filter not a ranking" part stands. The claim beside it — *"the band is
   validated, everything inside it saturated"* — described **n = 1 variant** and buried the
   phase's largest finding: two variants sat *below* `1/64` and saturated anyway, so the lower
   bound is disconfirmed with a measured 42–68% false-negative rate. **Superseded by the L1
   redesign** now recorded in `pre-registration.md` §4. Left visible rather than rewritten, because
   this is the record of what was believed when.

10. **PROPOSED — `CLAUDE.md` §15 gotcha 1.** *"Nothing hacks at 1.7B in 200 steps. The project's
    biggest risk... moved into Phase 0.4 (R1) so it surfaces in week 1"* describes a plan that has
    now executed. Rewrite to the measured result and what remains open: **retired for a trivial
    grader at 1B (15/15, ~8–40 steps); untested for `bisect`'s multi-step sandbox-mediated
    exploits**, which is the version that still deserves to be a gotcha.

### `[DELETE]`

11. **PROPOSED — remove `r1p_ordering_holds` from `onset_verdict`'s output entirely.** Renaming it
    made it honest but left the footgun in place: it is still a boolean about the discriminating
    pair sitting in the results dict, and `order_by_onset` and `order_our_base_rates_predict` are
    already there for anyone who wants to compare orderings by eye. The only correct consumer of a
    direction is `r1p_test`. One-line change plus a test deletion; held for the user's word because
    it is scoring logic (§7).

12. **EXECUTED during the phase — the publish gate on `assay-hackword`.** Inherited from Phase 0.2's
    caution about publishing `bisect` before the paper, and applied to R1 by reflex. Challenged with
    *"what is the irreversible thing that could happen if we publish"* and withdrawn: the env is a
    one-word grader with no novel contribution to scoop, and the free queue **requires** a public
    environment, so the gate was blocking the phase's funding model to protect nothing.
    *Trigger: without this, a rule is inherited across contexts on the strength of having been right
    once; with this, an inherited gate is re-derived against the artifact actually in front of it.*

**Considered for deletion and kept, with reasons** (§14 requires the consideration, not the
outcome):

- **L2, the positive control** (`pre-registration.md` §4) — R1 discharges it for `prime-rl` @ 1B, so
  deletion is tempting. Kept because a positive control certifies *a rig*, and Walk's rig is a
  different environment on a different model. Discharged-for-this-rig, not retired.
- **G3's ±50% band** — it produced a `partial` rather than a clean verdict. Kept: the looseness was
  deliberate and correctly anticipated that a different substrate moves magnitudes; a tighter band
  would have manufactured a failure, and §3.8 vindicates the reasoning.
- **`separated` in `r1p_test`'s output** — no longer the criterion. Kept as description, since
  "do the ranges overlap" is the single most legible summary of why a result is unresolved.

## Added after the three-reviewer pass (2026-08-06)

The pass is §12.6's counter-measure at a critical boundary, and it earned its place: two reviewers
converged independently on defects that had already reached the public ledger. Rules earned there,
all with triggers in `tasks/lessons.md`:

### `[ADD]` (continued)

13. **A screen is scored on its misses, not on the cases it admitted.**
    *Trigger: without this, "everything inside the band saturated" is written down as validation
    when it describes n=1 and the two out-of-band variants that saturated are the actual finding;
    with this, the disconfirming cases are counted first.*

14. **A threshold is checked for whether it encodes a measurement limit rather than a claim.**
    *Trigger: without this, `1/64` reads as "unreachable below here" when it is the resolution floor
    of a 64-sample screen; with this, the sampling budget and the scientific bound are separable.*

15. **The verdict written up is the one the analysis returned.**
    *Trigger: without this, a scorer prints UNRESOLVED and four documents say FALSIFIED, because
    self-falsification is a flattering story and a bias toward apparent rigour is still a bias; with
    this, the writeup and the committed output cannot diverge unnoticed.*

16. **Every claim names its metric when more than one exists.**
    *Trigger: without this, an unlabelled number drifts to whichever series makes the sentence work
    — R1 quoted train counts, train floors and a train-only narrative under an eval headline, each
    individually the stronger figure; with this, the drift is visible.*
